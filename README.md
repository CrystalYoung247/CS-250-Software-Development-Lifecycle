# CS-250: Software Development Lifecycle

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective and Java slideshow application from CS-250 at Southern New Hampshire University. The SNHU Travel project provided opportunities to interpret customer requirements, develop and test functionality, and evaluate how Agile practices influence software delivery.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I begin by identifying what users want to accomplish and translating those expectations into specific requirements. During the SNHU Travel project, I created user stories based on traveler preferences, including budget limits, vacation types, and personalized destination recommendations. Writing acceptance criteria helped me determine what each feature needed to accomplish and how its functionality could be tested. One important lesson came from testing personalized recommendations: although the slideshow displayed destination images and navigation worked, the application could not automatically update recommendations when preferences changed. This experience taught me to distinguish between a feature that functions and a user story that fully meets its intended requirements.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach development by understanding the requirements, organizing the work into manageable tasks, implementing functionality, and evaluating the results. While modifying the Java slideshow, I learned how changes to an application's interface can introduce problems that are not immediately apparent. Instructor feedback identified a layout conflict that prevented destination descriptions from appearing. Correcting the issue reinforced the importance of testing the complete user experience. In future development work, I intend to incorporate backlog refinement, short sprints, regular testing, and sprint retrospectives. These practices will help me manage changing priorities, identify problems earlier, and make improvements based on working results.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

A good team member takes responsibility for their work while recognizing how their decisions affect others. During our Group 5 Agile discussion, I participated as the Product Owner and asked how developers could share knowledge without interrupting sprint progress. A classmate suggested brief code reviews, occasional pair programming, and rotating smaller tasks. Considering those ideas showed me the value of listening to different perspectives rather than assuming one approach is best. I also learned that effective collaboration requires asking questions, communicating obstacles, and remaining open to feedback. Regardless of the Scrum role, I believe contributing to the team's shared goals requires accountability, respect, and a willingness to improve.
